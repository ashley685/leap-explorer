How settlement works

There's no term limit. Your agreement settles when you sell or transfer the home, move out for good, or pass away, or any time you choose to settle early, with no penalty. At settlement, the investor receives its share of your home's value at that time. That amount is capped: it can never be more than your investment payment grown at the annual cost cap, compounded each year. In the early years the cap usually sets the amount. Later, the share of your home's value tends to take over, and your effective annual cost comes down the longer you keep it. A $500 settlement fee, plus appraisal and lien release costs, is added at settlement.

The full explanation

This is a home equity investment agreement. You receive a lump sum today in exchange for a share of your home's future value. You keep ownership and keep living in your home, and you stay current on property taxes, insurance and any HOA dues.

There are no monthly payments and no interest, and there's no term limit. The agreement settles when you sell or transfer the home, permanently move out, or pass away, or whenever you decide to settle early, in part or in full, without penalty. Your cost is protected by an annualized cost cap, so even if your home's value climbs quickly, what you pay at settlement is limited.

Homeowners use it to fund long-term care, insurance, retirement income, or investments, and to avoid selling other assets at the wrong time. It's a good fit if you want cash without a new monthly bill. It isn't the right fit if you plan to sell within a year or two, or if you'd rather have the lowest possible cost and are comfortable with monthly payments.
